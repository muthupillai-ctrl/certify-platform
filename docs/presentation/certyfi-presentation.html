The problem

Paper and PDF certificates are easy to fake and slow to check

Easy to forge

A scanned certificate or PDF can be edited with ordinary tools, and the copy looks genuine.

Slow to verify

Employers and universities email or phone the issuing institution and wait days for an answer.

One person can issue

Without a second check, a single staff member can issue a certificate that should never exist.

What Certyfi is

One platform to design, issue, manage and verify

Two people for every certificate

One person prepares it and a different person approves it. The system blocks self-approval, even for administrators.

Proof that doesn't depend on us

Each certificate is signed with the institution's own key, using open international standards, and its history is timestamped on Bitcoin.

Many institutions, fully separate

Each institution has its own isolated workspace, users, branding and signing key.

The trust layer

Four independent layers, each stopping a different fraud

Official record

Stops edited PDFs: verification always shows the institution's own record.

Digital signature

Stops forgery: signed with the institution's own key, checkable with standard tools.

Tamper-evident audit trail

Stops silent changes: every action is chained to the one before it and can't be edited.

Bitcoin anchoring

Stops rewriting history, even by us: the audit trail is timestamped on Bitcoin.

Digital signature

How the digital signature works

1

Fingerprint

The certificate's details become a unique fingerprint. Change one letter and it no longer matches.

2

Seal

The institution's private key seals that fingerprint. No other key can make the same seal.

3

Check

Anyone checks the seal with the institution's public key. It passes only if nothing changed.

Private key: kept secret

One per institution, stored encrypted, used only inside the signing service. Never shown or sent anywhere.

Public key: open to all

Published for anyone to check signatures with standard tools such as OpenSSL. No Certyfi software needed.

Standard tools

Check a signature yourself

1

Copy three things

From the certificate's verification page: the public key, the signature and the fingerprint.

2

Run OpenSSL

Free, and included with macOS and Linux. No Certyfi software is involved.

3

Read the answer

"Verified OK" means genuine. Change one character and the check fails.

$ printf '%s' '5ca3a66a…f1ab16da' > fingerprint.txt

$ openssl base64 -d -A -in signature.txt -out signature.der

$ openssl dgst -sha256 -verify key.pem -signature signature.der fingerprint.txt

Verified OK

Why Bitcoin

A public record no one can rewrite

What is Bitcoin?

Besides being a currency, Bitcoin is a worldwide public ledger kept by thousands of independent computers. Anything written into it gets a date, and can never be changed or removed, by anyone.

We use it as a public notary, not as money: no cryptocurrency is bought, held or paid, and no one needs a Bitcoin wallet.

Not even we can rewrite history

Records can't be backdated or quietly changed afterwards.

Proof that outlives us

Anyone can check it with free, open tools, even without Certyfi.

Private by design

Only a digital fingerprint is recorded, never names or details.

Privacy and data protection

Personal data, handled with care

The institution stays in control

Each institution's data is kept separate and used only to issue and verify its certificates.

Only what a verifier needs

Public pages never show email addresses or internal notes.

Checkers stay anonymous

Who checks a certificate is never recorded, only that it was checked.

Erasure on request

Personal details can be erased without breaking the tamper-evident audit trail.

Nothing personal on Bitcoin

Only anonymous fingerprints are timestamped, never names or details.

Agreed with each institution

Data-protection terms, including where data is hosted, are agreed before it starts.

Designed around the principles of Nigeria's Data Protection Act 2023 and similar laws across Africa.
